When a failed scan looks like a closed job

A missing vacancy can mean the employer closed it or the scanner stopped early. TechJobsNL needs enough evidence to tell the difference.

Mohamed Halawa/22 Nov 2026

Imagine a careers site with two pages of vacancies. Yesterday both pages loaded. Today the first page loads and the second times out. A simple comparison with yesterday’s list would mark every vacancy on page two as closed. One network failure would become a hiring trend in your history.

TechJobsNL is a local tool I built to track vacancies at selected companies in the Netherlands. It keeps job history in SQLite, so a bad decision about a missing vacancy can outlive the scan that produced it. If the next scan succeeds, the same job might even appear to have closed and reopened overnight.

Decide one company at a time

A scan can finish in three states. A complete company scan can add jobs, update them, mark them closed or reopen them. An incomplete scan records diagnostics and keeps that company’s last trusted jobs. A failed scan does the same.

Consider a scan of two employers. The first returns a complete list with one new vacancy. The second returns only its first page. The first employer’s new vacancy can go into the database right away. The second employer keeps its previous list, including jobs from page two. Its partially fetched first page also waits for a complete scan. That gives the saved list a clear meaning at the cost of showing older information for that employer.

Follow the evidence back

The same distinction matters in the analytics view. Select a skill and you can inspect the stored vacancies that contributed to it. If a result surprises you, you can read the job descriptions and check which companies you chose to follow. The count describes the postings in this local dataset. Coverage changes when you follow different companies.

The most useful question in a code review of a new source adapter is often the smallest one. How does this particular careers site tell us we reached the end? The project keeps its source evidence alongside the code so that answer can be checked again when a site changes.
